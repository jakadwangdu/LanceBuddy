The Freelancer's Client Onboarding Checklist: From 'Yes' to Kickoff

The client said yes. Congratulations — now the real test begins. Most freelancers lose the momentum here: scope creeps, deposits stall, feedback arrives in seven different channels. This 15-step checklist turns "yes" into a clean, professional kickoff in under 48 hours.

Phase 1: Lock It Down (Day 0)

1. Send a "Yes, confirmed" email within 2 hours

Speed signals professionalism. Restate in writing what was agreed: scope, price, timeline, and payment terms. Even a short confirmation email becomes your reference point when "we never agreed to that" appears later.

2. Send the contract

A one-page agreement is enough: parties, scope, deliverables, price, payment schedule (typically 50% advance / 50% on delivery), revision limits (2 rounds, then billing), IP transfer on full payment, and termination terms. Attach it to the confirmation email as a PDF. If you don't have a template yet, build one now — you'll use it every month.

3. Collect the advance

Never start without the deposit. 50% is standard in India; 30% works for small jobs with trusted repeat clients. Create a UPI/QR or bank detail line in your contract so payment takes one click. If the client hesitates on the advance, that's a warning sign — talk, don't start.

4. Book the kickoff call

Offer two specific slots (e.g., "Thursday 4 PM or Friday 11 AM") rather than "whenever works." You're managing the timeline now.

Phase 2: Gather Everything (Day 1–2)

5. Send a requirements questionnaire

Keep it to one page and make it copy-pasteable: business goals, target customers, must-have features, likes/dislikes (3 links each), brand colors/fonts if any, content ownership. Most owners will answer in 20 minutes — and their answers are your scope protection.

6. Collect access & assets

Everything you need in one folder: logo files, photos, passwords (share via a secure channel, never in chat), domain and hosting logins, social media handles, and any existing analytics. Name the folder client-name-assets and ask them to add what's missing.

7. Define the single point of contact

Ask: "Who should I talk to for decisions, and who reviews the work?" One decision-maker prevents the committee problem where nothing gets approved. Write their name and email at the top of your notes.

8. Agree on the feedback channel

Pick one channel — WhatsApp for quick questions, email for formal approvals. State it plainly: "All change requests go here so nothing gets lost." This one rule eliminates 80% of freelancer chaos.

Phase 3: Plan the Work (Day 2–3)

9. Build a simple project plan

Five lines in an email: what happens this week, what they'll see when, what they need to provide and by when. Clients don't need Gantt charts — they need a calendar they can trust.

10. Define "done" for each milestone

Write acceptance criteria in plain language. For a website: "Homepage + 4 pages live on a preview link, mobile-correct, all forms working." When both sides agree on the definition of done, "almost done" arguments disappear.

11. Set the revision policy in writing

Two rounds of feedback per milestone, each round must arrive within 5 working days, and anything after round 2 is billed separately. Politely, once, in writing — then never renegotiate it live.

12. Create the project dashboard

Freelancers who track leads and projects in spreadsheets lose things. Move every active client into a pipeline tracker with status, next action, and last contact date — the free LanceBuddy tracker is built for exactly this, with CSV export for your records.

Phase 4: Kick Off & First Week (Day 3–7)

13. Hold the kickoff call

30 minutes max. Walk through the plan, confirm priorities ("most important thing to nail in week 1?"), and let them talk. Take notes in front of them — it signals you'll remember.

14. Deliver something visible in the first 5 days

Show momentum early. A wireframe, a mood board, the homepage concept, the first email draft — anything they can react to. Early visible progress compounds trust for the whole project.

15. Send the Friday update

Every Friday, one short email: what shipped this week, what's next, anything I need from you. It takes 5 minutes, and it's the highest-leverage habit in client management. Clients stop chasing you, because you're already ahead of them.

The Anti-Checklist (What NOT to Do)

  • Don't start work before the advance clears your bank.
  • Don't accept "can you just start, we'll sort payment next week."
  • Don't take "I'm not technical, you decide" at face value — get their preference on the 3 decisions that matter most.
  • Don't do "one small extra" for free in week 1 and charge for the same thing in week 3 — clients remember consistency, not generosity.
Remember: onboarding is where projects win or die. A client who felt organized and respected during day one will defend your invoices, refer you to their network, and never haggle your renewal rate.
