10 Cold Email Templates That Actually Get Replies (With Examples)

The average freelancer sends a cold email, gets silence, and concludes "cold email doesn't work." It works — your template was the problem. Here are 10 templates with real subject lines that regularly pull 15–25% reply rates, plus the psychology of why each one works.

Why Most Cold Emails Fail

Before the templates, fix the four mistakes that kill 90% of cold emails:

  • You lead with you, not them. "I'm a freelance developer" is not a reason for anyone to keep reading. The first sentence must be about the recipient's business.
  • You pitch, then disappear. One email and done is not outreach — it's a lottery ticket. Follow-up is where 60% of replies come from.
  • You ask for too much. "Would you like to hop on a call this week?" is a big commitment. Ask for a one-line reply instead.
  • You're generic. "Hi Sir/Madam" and templated flattery are instantly recognizable. One specific detail about the business changes everything.

Every template below fixes all four. Personalize the bracketed [details], keep the body under 120 words, and send at 7:30–9:30 AM local time (Tuesday–Thursday works best).

The Universal Cold Email Structure

If you remember one thing: Hook → Specific value → Tiny ask. The hook is one sentence about their business (not your service). The value is a concrete outcome or observation. The ask is a yes/no question answerable in one line.

1. The Compliment + Observation (Best for Local Businesses)

Works for anything: restaurants, gyms, clinics, travel agencies. You found the business, noticed something specific, and offer one fix.

Subject: Quick thought about [Business Name]'s website Hi [Owner Name], I came across [Business Name] while searching for [service] in [city], and I really liked your [specific thing — menu, photos, location, reviews]. One thing I noticed: your site doesn't show up well on mobile, and it takes about 8 seconds to load. On mobile, that usually means losing [estimated %] of visitors before they even see your menu. I build fast, mobile-friendly websites for businesses like yours. I'd be happy to share 2–3 quick fixes that cost nothing. Would it help if I sent them over? Best, [Your Name] [Link to 1 relevant portfolio example]

Why it works: it references their actual business, offers a no-cost observation, and the ask ("would it help if I sent them over?") is nearly impossible to say no to. This single template has the highest reply rate of anything in this list.

2. The Referral-Radius (Leverage a Neighbor)

People trust proximity. If you've done work for a business nearby, say so.

Subject: I work with [Nearby Business] down the street Hi [Owner Name], I've been helping [Nearby Business, same street] with their [website / social media / branding] for the last few months. I saw [Business Name] doesn't have a [website / online booking / active Instagram], and I thought you might be interested in how it's been working for them — they're getting roughly [X] enquiries per month from it now. Happy to share the numbers if useful. Best, [Your Name]

3. The Value Math (For Web Dev & Agencies)

When the business has an obvious revenue metric (booking volume, footfall, average ticket size), show simple math.

Subject: Rough estimate for [Business Name] Hi [Owner Name], I noticed [Business Name] gets strong reviews on Google Maps ([number] reviews, [rating] stars) but doesn't appear for searches like "[city] [service]" — you're being outranked by [Competitor Name] in your own area. A small improvement to your Google Business Profile and website could mean 10–15 extra enquiries per month. At your average ticket size of [X], that's roughly [Y] in monthly revenue. I've done this for similar businesses. Want me to send you a 3-point plan? Best, [Your Name]

4. The "I Fixed It Already" (Design & Marketing)

Do 30 minutes of free work first. Show a mockup or a fixed version of one of their assets. It's the strongest proof there is.

Subject: A quick redesign of your homepage hero Hi [Owner Name], I redesigned the hero section of your homepage as a sample — took me 45 minutes, attached it here. The current version leads with "Welcome to [Business Name]" and buries your actual offer. This version leads with your strongest service and adds one clear button. If you like it, I can apply the same treatment to the rest of the page for [price]. If not, keep it as a freebie. Best, [Your Name]

5. The Anti-Pitch (For High-Ticket / B2B)

For software companies, clinics, and larger firms where a direct pitch feels pushy.

Subject: Not a pitch — just a question Hi [Name], Not a pitch, just curious: how are you currently handling [problem related to your service] at [Company]? I've been studying how companies like yours manage this and I'm building a short guide around it. Happy to share it with you when it's ready — you might find it useful. Either way, have a good week. Best, [Your Name]

6. The One-Sentence Email (Busy Owners)

For owners who clearly run a one-person show (check their Google Maps listing — if there's no website, they're busy).

Subject: Website? Hi [Name] — saw [Business Name] on Google Maps; noticed you don't have a website. I build simple ones for businesses like yours in [city], starting at [price]. Want details? — [Your Name]

Why it works: busy owners reply to emails that take 5 seconds to read. Subject line "Website?" followed by one tight sentence reads like a text message.

7. The Follow-Up #1 (48 hours later)

Subject: Re: [Original subject] Hi [Name], Just bumping this to the top of your inbox — completely understand if it's not a priority right now. If it helps, here's the one thing I'd change on your site first: [specific, actionable one-liner]. No strings attached. Best, [Your Name]

8. The Follow-Up #2 (5–7 days later)

Subject: [Business Name] / quick one Hi [Name], Last one from me. If you ever need [service] in the future, my details are below. I've attached a 1-page summary of what I do and what it costs, so there's no back-and-forth needed. Either way, wishing you and [Business Name] a great quarter. Best, [Your Name]

9. The Response Handler (When they reply "send details")

Most freelancers fumble here. Have this ready to paste the moment they reply.

Subject: Re: [Original subject] Hi [Name], Great to hear from you! Quick summary: What I do: [one line about your service] For businesses like: [their type] Price: [transparent range, e.g. INR 15,000–40,000] Timeline: [e.g. 2–3 weeks] How we'd start: [free 15-min call / free sample task] Want to grab 15 minutes this week? I'm free [day] or [day]. Best, [Your Name]

10. The LinkedIn + Email Combo

Send a connection request on LinkedIn with a short note, then follow with the email 2 days later. The name recognition alone lifts reply rates by a third.

LinkedIn note: Hi [Name] — I came across [Business Name] and have an idea that could [benefit]. Would love to connect and share it. — [Your Name] Then, 2 days later, email: Subject: Following up on my LinkedIn note Hi [Name], I sent you a LinkedIn note earlier this week about [Business Name] — this is easier to reply to. [Paste the body of Template #1 or #3] Best, [Your Name]

How to Send These (Tooling)

  • Find leads: use the free LanceBuddy Scout Tool to build a list of local businesses with verified source links — verify each listing before emailing.
  • Personalize: open the source link, grab the owner's name from the listing or website footer, and reference one real detail.
  • Send: Gmail or Outlook for the first 20–50 emails. Scale only after you've validated a template.
  • Track: reply rate is the only metric that matters. Below 5% means your list or hook is weak — change one variable at a time.
Realistic expectations: a 15–25% reply rate and a 3–8% booking rate from 100 emails is an excellent outcome. If your emails aren't performing, the problem is usually the list (wrong audience) — never email a lead you haven't verified.

Final Checklist Before You Send

  1. Recipient's name is correct and their business actually exists (check the source link)
  2. First sentence is about them, not you
  3. One specific detail proves you actually looked
  4. Body under 120 words
  5. Ask is answerable in one line
  6. Follow-ups are already scheduled (day 2 and day 7)
